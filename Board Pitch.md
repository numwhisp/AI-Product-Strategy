## Board Pitch

**Thesis (1 sentence):** We can turn the moment a customer opens a bank account into a qualified insurance conversation by identifying likely coverage gaps and putting a licensed broker in position to act, but the investment only makes sense if the pilot proves the signal converts better than doing nothing.

**The case:**

1. **Why now:** The timing case is not yet sufficiently developed in the strategy. The opportunity is clear—insurance discovery is being moved into a high-intent banking workflow, while AI makes contextual detection and explanation inexpensive—but the strategy does not document a specific change in the last 6–12 months that creates a defensible timing window. Before presenting this to an external board, this needs evidence: a market, distribution, regulatory, or competitor development that makes account-opening the right moment to act now rather than later.
2. **What's defensible:** The named moat is **Contextual Moat + Platform Exposure**. The product is embedded inside a regulated bank or credit union's actual account-opening flow and connects the signal to a licensed broker relationship. That distribution and regulated workflow are harder to reproduce than the model itself. But the data advantage is currently weak: Domain Context scores 2/5, because the system relies on proxy bank-transaction signals rather than proprietary risk data. Verisk or another data vendor could materially narrow that gap through licensing, while Q2/Jack Henry could eventually bundle comparable capability into the core banking workflow. The intended long-term defense is the broker correction and network intelligence loop—not the underlying model.
3. **The economics:** **~84.4% AI-adjusted gross margin** is the current modeled economics, with AI inference representing only a small portion of total cost; AI COGS is approximately $0.163–$0.18 per user per month, and modeled margin remains approximately 94% at the AI-COGS layer under a 3x inference-cost scenario. The $2,500/month base fee plus $0.75 per broker-claimed AI lead sits on top of the existing $150 bound-policy commission. The important point is that inference economics do not currently threaten the model—the larger cost is human review. The outcome-based alternative ($300 per AI-attributed bind with no base fee) could improve the sales proposition but materially increases revenue risk.

**The risks:**

1. **Trust / failure modes:** The reliability contract targets 90–95% accuracy, less than 1% hallucination, p95 latency below 800ms, and drift below 0.5% over four weeks. The most credible high-severity failure already found is not hypothetical: red-teaming showed that loan refinances can be interpreted as new-loan events, creating false positives for customers who may already have coverage. That pattern is now part of the golden dataset, and the system requires human review below 90% confidence, for joint-account ambiguity, and for repeat-dispute customers. The front-page risk is therefore a systematically wrong insurance recommendation reaching customers at scale; the control is suppression/review rather than autonomous customer contact. That is appropriate for the current decision-support model, but the 10-row golden dataset must become the targeted ~150-row production-quality evaluation set before scaling.
2. **Scale / governance:** At 10x usage, inference cost is not the primary concern; operational review, workflow integration, correction capture, and governance are. The current system cannot contact customers, override compliance suppression, or retrain without AI/ML lead approval. The larger weakness is that the learning system is not yet closed: broker corrections are captured but are not automatically connected to retraining, and auto/home/life signals do not yet share learning. The current Responsible AI maturity is 15/25, with each of Compliance, Transparency, Fairness, Privacy, and Accountability at 3/5. The strategy therefore supports scaling the workflow only after the correction loop and operational tracking are automated—not scaling autonomy.
3. **Competitive:** The most dangerous scenario is not simply a competitor launching a similar AI feature; it is a platform consolidating the workflow and the data underneath it. Microsoft combining Copilot for Financial Services with Verisk-class data is the sharpest near-term threat because it could attack the same budget line without requiring the bank to adopt another standalone product. The roadmap's response is to license comparable risk data and build the shared cross-domain learning layer. But the ultimate kill criterion remains empirical: **if a real partner pilot does not produce a higher flagged-gap-to-bind conversion than the baseline of unprompted purchases from the same customer base, we should kill the bet because the underlying signal is not working.**

**The ask:** **Not yet board-ready.** The strategy does not specify a defensible dollar amount, headcount allocation, or funding source, so those numbers should not be invented for the board. The immediate proposal should be a **gated 1–3 month validation investment** tied to three deliverables: close the correction loop, fix refinance false positives, and validate the signal against a live partner baseline; the next funding gate should require evidence that conversion improves and that the Domain Context vulnerability can be reduced through licensed data. The strategy also does not identify what existing work would be paused to fund this effort; that tradeoff needs to be named explicitly before the ask is presented. If those conditions cannot be quantified, the board should receive a validation proposal—not a scale proposal.
